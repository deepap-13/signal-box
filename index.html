Seven tickets, four tracks.

You run the desk where field problems get routed before anyone acts on them. Each ticket describes an NGO partway into a public-education programme — and a choice about where to put its next effort.

Every ticket offers the same four numbered tracks, each described for that specific situation. Read what's actually happening, then send it down the track that fits — not the one that feels the most familiar.

1 · Intervention-AnchoredRide a specific programme forward, and build a skill that outlasts it.
2 · Reform-FirstNo programme yet. Build the institution's own capacity, directly.
3 · HybridKeep the programme, but fix the specific piece blocking it.
4 · Collective ActionNo single actor can close this. Convene the ones who can.

Why four tracks

Drawn from Mantra4Change's field experience and research on NGOs and public-system reform. Each pathway works at a different depth below the problem that actually got reported.

Patterns

The repeated routine a role performs — how a training gets planned, how a review gets run. Pathway 1 builds these deliberately, using a live programme as the vehicle.

Structures

Decision rights, missing roles, incentives, protected time. Pathway 2 builds an institution's capacity to originate its own response; Pathway 3 repairs one named structural piece blocking a programme already in motion.

Mental models

Who believes this is their job, and what's considered normal. Pathway 4 shifts this across actors who don't report to each other, by building one shared goal and joint accountability.
